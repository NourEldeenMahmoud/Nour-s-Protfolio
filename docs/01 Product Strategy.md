# Portfolio Product Strategy

## Product Purpose

Build a portfolio that proves Nour can understand a problem, design a system, and deliver a useful solution. Code and technology support that story; they are not the story by themselves.

The portfolio must communicate four qualities:

- **Specialized:** focused on .NET backend and full-stack application development.
- **Versatile:** able to work across web, desktop, mobile, games, automation, and technical education.
- **Fast-learning:** able to enter unfamiliar domains and produce working results.
- **Engineering-minded:** makes requirements, tradeoffs, architecture, validation, and delivery visible.

## Positioning

### Working headline

> Software engineer focused on .NET backend and full-stack applications, building solutions across products, platforms, and technical domains.

### Supporting message

Nour has broad technical range, but the portfolio should not present every skill as equal. .NET is the professional center. MEAN, Unity, Flutter, desktop applications, AI-assisted workflows, and knowledge sharing demonstrate adaptability around that center.

### Claims to avoid

- Do not claim seniority or production scale without evidence.
- Do not call every repository a flagship project.
- Do not present team output as solo output.
- Do not present a fork as an independently created application.
- Do not call proposed AI workflows operational products.
- Do not describe learning-course games as wholly original without documenting the course and asset provenance.

## Audiences

The portfolio serves three audiences, but not through one generic hierarchy.

| Audience  | Main question                                                        | Evidence to prioritize                                   | Primary action               |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Recruiter | Can Nour succeed in a junior .NET backend or full-stack role?        | Role, .NET depth, project ownership, teamwork, CV        | Review work and download CV  |
| Client    | Can Nour turn my problem into a dependable custom software solution? | Problem framing, outcomes, process, scope, communication | Start a project conversation |
| Developer | How does Nour reason, build, validate, and learn?                    | Architecture, tradeoffs, code, postmortems, tutorials    | Explore technical depth      |

The audience selector is prominent but optional. Visitors who skip it enter a complete shared experience.

## Release Strategy

### First release

- Shared default experience.
- Complete Recruiter experience.
- English and Arabic support.
- Five complete, evidence-backed case studies: BuildSense, Bookify, Blood Bank Platform, How to Train Your AI, and CinemaVerse.
- About, CV, contact, and a curated knowledge preview.
- Desktop-first visual experience with a complete simplified mobile version.
- Short cinematic introduction before the audience choice. It is immediately skippable, shown once by default, bypassed for returning visitors, and replaced for reduced-motion users.
- Two launch choices only: “I’m hiring” and “Explore everything.” Client and Developer choices stay hidden until their complete experiences exist.

### Later releases

- Full Client experience.
- Full Developer experience.
- “How I Work” tutorials.
- Knowledge product integration after its hosting model is decided.
- Optional gamification or discovery layer after usability validation.
- Interactive CV only if it solves a proven visitor need.

## MVP Experience Strategy

- `/explore` follows an engineering story: positioning, selected work, problem-solving process, range, knowledge preview, About, and contact.
- `/recruiter` begins with role fit and proof, then prioritizes .NET work, CV, and contact.
- Recruiter is a distinct visual world, not only reordered copy.
- Shared and Recruiter case studies use the same verified project facts but different presentations.
- Visitors change journeys by returning to the audience selector rather than through a persistent mode switcher.

## Visual Prototype Shortlist

The final art direction remains undecided. Before selection, prototype exactly these two candidates:

1. **Project Multiverse:** every flagship project becomes a distinct domain-specific world connected by one engineering core.
2. **Cinematic Product Stories:** every project becomes a high-impact visual chapter built around directed media and storytelling.

Digital Workshop is not part of the prototype phase.

## Success Criteria

The first release succeeds when a visitor can answer these questions without guessing:

1. What kind of engineer is Nour?
2. What did Nour personally contribute?
3. Which work demonstrates .NET depth?
4. Which work demonstrates range and learning speed?
5. How does Nour approach a problem beyond writing code?
6. What should the visitor do next?

## Product Principles

- **Evidence before adjectives.** Show a decision, artifact, result, or contribution instead of saying “innovative.”
- **Depth before inventory.** Five explained projects beat nineteen repository cards.
- **Range with hierarchy.** Breadth supports the .NET focus rather than competing with it.
- **Spectacle with purpose.** Animation explains systems, creates transitions, or rewards exploration.
- **No blocked content.** Audience modes and future gamification must never hide essential evidence.
- **Honest attribution.** Every case study names its context and Nour’s role.
- **Bilingual parity.** English and Arabic expose equivalent facts and actions.
- **Graceful reduction.** Mobile and reduced-capability devices keep all content and actions even when visual scenes are simplified.
