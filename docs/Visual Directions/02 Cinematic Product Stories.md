# Cinematic Product Stories

## Thesis

Each project is a short visual film with its own pacing, atmosphere, and dramatic question. The shared portfolio acts as a title sequence; case studies become chapters.

## Visual Language

- Oversized type, carefully directed screenshots, full-bleed video, and bold negative space.
- Project-specific art direction instead of one repeated card system.
- Editorial captions and evidence overlays keep the story factual.
- Transitions borrow from title design rather than generic fade-and-slide animation.

## Signature Moments

- Audience choice changes the opening cut and narration order.
- BuildSense begins with the confusion of fragmented Egyptian hardware listings, then resolves into a product flow.
- Bookify turns one reservation into a visual sequence from search to payment confirmation.
- How to Train Your AI can use game footage and interactive narrative beats.

## Motion

- Scroll choreography controls cuts, focus, scale, and layered depth.
- Use pinned scenes sparingly and always provide skip controls.
- Prefer authored media and typography over generic particles or 3D blobs.
- Mobile becomes a clean vertical edit with compressed clips and still frames.

## Typography and Color

- Display type can change by project while body type stays consistent.
- Color grading and media treatment establish each chapter.
- Arabic titles need equal visual authority, not smaller fallback typography.

## Audience Adaptation

- Recruiter: shorter cut with role and evidence surfaced early.
- Client: outcome-led narrative with collaboration and process.
- Developer: extended cut with diagrams and technical interludes.

## Risks

- Requires excellent screenshots, video, copy, and art direction.
- Heavy media can damage load time and maintainability.
- Spectacle can bury ownership details if the script is weak.

## Fit

Best direction for the requested “flexing my muscles” feeling when high-quality project media is available.
