# Adaptive Audience Worlds

## Thesis

The optional audience choice does more than reorder sections. It transforms the portfolio’s visual grammar, pacing, navigation, and storytelling while preserving one factual project model.

## Visual Language

- Shared brand nucleus with three clearly different worlds.
- Recruiter world: precise, fast, evidence-forward, and highly navigable.
- Client world: outcome-led, confident, warmer, and solution-oriented.
- Developer world: exploratory, dense on demand, diagrammatic, and instructional.
- Default world blends the shared elements without pretending to optimize for everyone.

## Signature Moments

- The opening selector previews each world before selection.
- Switching modes visibly reconfigures the current page rather than restarting the site.
- The same Bookify project transforms from role proof to business workflow to technical architecture.
- Language and audience transitions share one coherent transformation system.

## Motion

- Layout reflow is the main animation: sections reprioritize and evidence changes depth.
- Color, typography scale, rhythm, and navigation change with the mode.
- Avoid three unrelated websites; transformations need visible family resemblance.
- Mobile keeps mode differences mostly in hierarchy, copy, and color rather than expensive scenes.

## Typography and Color

- One body family and one structural grid across worlds.
- Different display treatment and accent systems per audience.
- Arabic mode receives designed RTL transformations for all three worlds.

## Risks

- Highest implementation, localization, testing, and maintenance cost.
- Visitors may miss content unique to another mode.
- Can become a gimmick if differences are only cosmetic.

## Fit

Most literal fulfillment of the approved long-term three-experience strategy, but should be phased after the shared and Recruiter foundation.
